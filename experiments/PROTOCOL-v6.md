# Attention precision and complete width comparison, v6

This is a prospective engineering experiment following the retained v5 numerical
failures. The intervention is higher-precision attention-score accumulation and
centering at widths 512 and 1024. V1–v5 data, failed criteria, source identities,
and releases remain unchanged. No speed or accuracy improvement is required for
an observation to be retained.

## Intervention and questions

The native wide-head kernel converts the stored FP32 queries and keys to FP64,
accumulates their dot products in FP64, and retains double precision through
scaling, finding the causal maximum, and subtracting that maximum. Only centered
scores are rounded to FP32 for the existing exponential, normalization, and value
mixing. Parameters, activations, probabilities, gradients, and optimizer state
remain stored in FP32. Wide attention backward also uses FP64 for the gradient
with respect to attention weights, its contraction, softmax derivative, scaled score derivatives, and complete causal
query-gradient accumulation; key-gradient contributions are added in FP64 before
FP32 storage, while value gradients retain their FP32 order. This extension was
required by the unchanged development gradient checks after the forward-only
candidate exposed cancellation. Initial failed development records are retained.
This is selective higher-precision accumulation, not an entirely FP32 arithmetic
path. The smaller-width and generic fallback kernels
retain their existing behavior. The real-valued model equations are unchanged.

The questions are whether this removes the diagnosed score-rounding weakness,
whether it satisfies a declared FP64 accuracy requirement at evolved states, and
what it costs in complete matched training and forward execution. An unchanged
PyTorch implementation is measured anew in the same campaign. Historical timing
differences cannot isolate the cost of the intervention from machine conditions.

## Fixed workload and execution

Measure **all six widths: 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024**. Three seeds (42, 43, 44),
two timing repeats, and both engines produce **72 complete fits**. Architecture,
initialization, data, schedules, learning rate, optimizer settings, and checkpoint
selection are exactly those in [v5](PROTOCOL-v5.md): one decoder block, four heads,
context 32, feed-forward width 2W, ASCII-128, eight epochs, batch 16, and 1,024 Adam
updates. Each fit processes 524,288 training targets from the same 65,536-target
training partition; validation and test each contain 8,192 targets. The best
epoch validation NLL selects the checkpoint; first epoch wins ties.

The corpus and previous test outcomes are already exposed. This remains a reused
engineering benchmark, not fresh assessment evidence. Test outcomes may not guide
kernel development, seed selection, hyperparameters, training duration, or error
thresholds. V5 failures may be regression examples, but admission also includes
independently generated stress cases and both engines' newly evolved states.

Run the deterministic balanced/reversed order generated by `study_order("v6")`.
Only one measured process runs at a time, on AC power, with one CPU compute thread
and 15 seconds between fits. No builds, validation, profiling, or other project
numerical work runs concurrently with timing. Record before/after power, thermal,
and load information. A bad process, changed source/input/binary, or invalid power
condition stops execution and preserves evidence; retries use new paths.

Training time includes forward/backward, Adam, finite checks, epoch validation,
checkpoint selection/writes, and bookkeeping. Packing remains inside the timer.
Setup, final test, and inference samples are excluded. Forward latency is batch
one over the complete context, after 20 warmups and with 100 samples. It excludes
loss, output-probability softmax, sampling, token loading, and I/O. RSS includes
framework/runtime and data; retain training-boundary and final process peaks.

## Admission before measurement

Commit implementation, validators, reporting code, and this protocol before the
first fit. Require clean source and authenticated current binaries/layouts at all
six widths for automatic, forced NEON, and hardware-supported forced SME policies.
Retain original primitive, common-state, optimizer, runtime-control, causal,
buffer/ABI, and malformed-input gates, including explicit legacy trajectory
failures under the existing wide-model contract. Smaller widths retain their
strict trajectory contract. No old tolerance is widened.

The additional `v6-fp64-centered-attention-v1` precision contract authenticates
the complete named checks, deterministic fixtures, and independent NumPy FP64
equations in `transformer/precision.py`. Synthetic inputs separately vary large
common score offsets and small score gaps, include contrasting values,
cancellation, causal prefixes, and nonzero incoming gradients. They test both
forward and backward against FP64 using exactly representable stored FP32 inputs.
Independent PyTorch FP64 calculations cross-check the oracle. Development and
evaluation random streams are distinct and fixed in the committed source.

Use the existing forward limits **atol 2e-5, rtol 2e-4** and gradient limits
**atol 3e-5, rtol 3e-3** for the added native accuracy checks. Probability and mass
checks retain **atol 2e-7, rtol 2e-6**; independent FP64 oracles use atol/rtol 1e-10.
There are 23 cases at each wide width and three at each smaller width. Development
seed 20261101 and admission seed 20261102 are distinct. No criterion may
change after formal measurement starts. FP32 framework disagreements on the new
stress cases are explicit diagnostics, not concealed passes or a reason to reject
a more accurate native answer. Complete diagnostic records and their failures
are retained separately from blocking admission checks.

## Post-training accuracy, compatibility, and evidence integrity

At every selected checkpoint from **both training engines**, recompute every
test and validation row using native, production PyTorch FP32, and independent FP64 equations with
identical saved parameters. Replay the originating engine with its original batch
semantics and require exact reproduction of saved logits. Verify selected epoch,
own-engine validation history, finite states, optimizer count, file identities,
and exact within-engine repeat equality. These are evidence-integrity checks.

Evaluate native and PyTorch separately against FP64 at the unchanged forward
limit. Also retain native/PyTorch FP32 compatibility comparisons as diagnostics,
including actual false values. Report probability differences, NLL differences,
and argmax changes alongside raw-logit errors. FP64 arithmetic evaluates the same
equations at the exact stored FP32 parameters; it is an accuracy reference, not a
new trained model or a replacement for saved-prediction quality measurements.

Every report exposes four separate booleans:

- `evidence_integrity_passed`
- `native_fp64_accuracy_passed`
- `python_fp64_accuracy_passed`
- `cross_implementation_fp32_compatibility_passed`

A fully numerically qualified comparison requires integrity and **both** FP64
accuracy checks. If only the baseline fails accuracy, use
`baseline_precision_limit`; a native failure uses `native_precision_limit`.
Broken evidence uses `audit_failed`. Always retain complete measurements and all
widths, even when numerical qualification fails. A native accuracy improvement
can be reported separately from whether the matched comparison fully qualifies.
No status may imply that the historical PyTorch-compatibility audit passed.

## Analysis and publication

For each width, report the geometric mean of six paired PyTorch/native training
ratios. Forward ratios compare mean per-fit medians. Average repeats within seed
before reporting mean and sample SD across three seeds. Do not pool widths or
treat repeats as independent quality seeds. Preserve all 72 fits and **589,824
test prediction rows**, with baselines, paired quality differences, checkpoints,
Adam states, histories, latency samples, conditions, hashes, and source revisions.

Use static figures with qualification clearly labeled. Lead the repository README
with the hardware/precision idea, put results later, and link the full evidence.
Explain unfavorable results and the precision/performance tradeoff. Keep v5's
failed report and release intact. Claims remain limited to this short-context CPU
model family against eager PyTorch; no GPU, compiled-PyTorch, long-context, energy,
cached-decoding, or production-serving conclusions follow.
